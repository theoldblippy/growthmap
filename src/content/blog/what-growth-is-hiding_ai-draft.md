---
title: "What Growth Is Hiding From You"
description: "When the numbers are climbing, every operating sin gets covered up. The best time to audit your business is while it's still working."
date: 2026-06-15
draft: true
---

There's a line I keep coming back to lately: growth covers over a myriad of sins.

When a business is growing, almost nothing feels urgent. Revenue is climbing, the team is busy, the board is happy. The onboarding process that lives entirely in one person's head? We'll write it down later. The sales team that closes more on personality than on any real method? It's working, leave it alone. The dashboard with three numbers nobody actually trusts? Green enough. Ship it.

Growth is the best disguise a broken system ever had. And the bill always comes due.

## The numbers look identical right up until they don't

Here's the trap. A company growing because it built a real system and a company growing in spite of a missing one produce the exact same scoreboard for a while. Same revenue line going up and to the right. Same happy board deck. You genuinely cannot tell the two apart from the outside.

Then something slows down. A big deal slips, a market shifts, a quarter comes in soft. And every sin you papered over while you were winning shows up on the table at the same time, fully lit, waiting on you.

That's the cruel part. The problems surface at the worst possible moment to fix them. Cash is tighter. The team is nervous. You're trying to repair five structural things at once, under pressure, with less room to maneuver than you had a year ago when everything was fine and you chose not to look.

## The only honest move is to look while it's good

The instinct most operators have is to dig into the business when it's on fire. That's human. Pain creates focus. But by the time there's a fire, your options have shrunk.

The better discipline is to audit hardest when the numbers are good. When you have margin, cash, and a calm team, that's exactly when you should be asking the uncomfortable questions. Does this actually work, or are we just growing fast enough to hide that it doesn't? If our growth rate dropped in half tomorrow, what's the first thing that would break? Who is the one person whose departure would take a whole process out the door with them?

Nobody wants to ask those questions when things are going well. It feels like inventing problems. It's the opposite. It's the only time you get to fix them cheaply.

## Lean into the red

A mentor drilled something into me years ago that I still feel the pull to ignore: lean into the red.

When a number looks bad, the reflex is to soften it. Bury it under a friendlier metric. Wrap it in a hopeful story about next quarter. Report green because green gets you left alone and red gets you questions you don't have answers to yet.

The strongest teams I've seen do the opposite. They put the ugly number on the table early, they say it out loud, and they build the plan to fix it right there in the room. A dashboard full of green that nobody in the room actually believes is just a problem you've quietly agreed to be surprised by later.

Intellectual honesty about where the business really is costs you a little pride in the moment and saves you a crisis down the road. That's a trade worth making every single time.

## You can't save your way to success

When growth finally does stall, the first reflex is almost always to cut. Trim the spend, freeze the hires, tighten the belt. Cutting is fine. Sometimes it's necessary. It buys you time.

But cutting doesn't build anything. You can't save your way to success. If you spend the runway you just bought yourself only on cutting deeper, all you've done is run the same broken machine on a smaller budget until it stalls anyway. The time has to go toward fixing the engine that actually brings money in. The product. The pitch. The motion that turns a stranger into a customer.

## Build it so the next slowdown isn't an emergency

This is the whole idea behind a real operating system: a business you've pressure-tested while it's healthy, so that when the inevitable slow quarter arrives, it lands as a manageable dip instead of a five-alarm scramble.

Growth will always cover your sins for a while. That's exactly why you can't trust it to tell you the truth. A business that only works when it's growing is still just a bet.

Look under the hood while the engine's running smooth. That's when you can actually fix something.
